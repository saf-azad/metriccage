This site used to assert excellent calibration and mediocre discrimination, and then show neither the forecast distribution nor a baseline. Every chart below exists because that sentence deserved evidence — two of them ended up contradicting things published elsewhere on this site.

01 · the central claim, as arithmetic

Calibration and discrimination are two terms of one equation.

Murphy’s decomposition, quantile bins: Brier 0.2286 = reliability 0.0033 − resolution 0.0240 + uncertainty 0.2496 · Brier skill over the base rate +0.084

The decomposition, to scale

Reliability near zero is the calibration half. Resolution of 0.024 against a ceiling of 0.250 is the discrimination half — and the gap the roadmap targets.

The forecast distribution nobody showed

The isotonic calibrator maps 695 blended probabilities onto 24 distinct values — one of them, 0.5621, covers 207 fights (30% of the test set).

57% of forecasts sit within 10 pts of a coin flip · 28% outside 30–70

The calibrator earns its calibration honestly — log-loss 0.6463 against 0.6498 for Platt scaling and 0.6512 uncalibrated — but it buys it by quantising the output, so the model has less usable resolution than a continuous probability implies.

02 · value added

A four-model ensemble is worth exactly what it beats.

The first question a reviewer asks about an ensemble is whether it outperforms its own best component. The second is what each component actually contributes. The site used to answer neither, and showed the fitted weights as if they were a result.

The baseline ladder — test log-loss, lower is better

Every stream isotonic-calibrated on 2023; the shipped ensemble beats each of its own components.

analysis/model_eval.json · ladder

Leave-one-out ablation — Δ log-loss when a stream is dropped

Weights and calibrator refit on validation each time. A stream with 12.4% of the weight is contributing nothing.

Elo’s signal already lives inside the other streams as features · analysis/model_eval.json · ablation

The two charts answer different questions and both are needed. The ladder says the blend is worth building; the ablation says one quarter of it is not. Elo keeps its weight because the least-squares fit is solved on validation data, where its contribution is marginally positive — on the test years it is not.

03 · what actually drives it

Two ways to rank a feature. They disagree completely.

XGBoost’s built-in gain is biased toward continuous, high-cardinality splits, unstable across refits — and it was read off one stream carrying 35% of the ensemble weight to support a claim about the whole system.

Gain said: style outranks output

Gain counts how much each split improved the training objective. That rewards features which can be cut many different ways, and it is measured on the very data the trees were fitted to — so a feature can rank first for reasons that have nothing to do with predicting an unseen fight.

#1 Archetype cluster (red) · #2 Archetype cluster (blue) — the ranking the site used to headline.

Permutation on held-out data says: age, pace, experience

Permutation asks a blunter question: shuffle one feature on data the model has never seen, and measure how much worse the forecast gets. Nothing survives that test on the strength of how it looked during training.

Δ log-loss when permuted: age +0.049 · pace mismatch +0.038 · third and fourth, the two fighters’ UFC fight counts. The archetype clusters do not reach the top 30.

Both experience counts ranking top-4 is the debutant blind spot arriving from a different direction.

What the Macau anecdote is actually worth

One card is thirteen data points, most of which the model was never in a position to score. It cannot carry a structural claim about the system, and it should not have been asked to — the population split on the left is the same claim with enough evidence behind it to stand up.

Of thirteen bouts, four were UFC debutants the model cannot score and one was a no-contest. On the eight it scored it went 5 — against an expected 5.33 from its own probabilities (central 80%: 4–7).

06 · named, not hidden

What this page still cannot show you.

Four charts that ought to be here and are not. Each is missing for a stated reason rather than an accidental one.

The closing line

No odds corpus exists over the test window, so the single most informative chart — model log-loss against the market on the same 695 fights — is the one this page does not have. Until it does, “beats the market” is a claim nobody here has earned.

Method of victory

The two-stage head reports AUC ≈ 0.56 in prose and appears in no chart. A confusion matrix would be more convincing than the confession, and rather less flattering.

Pre-UFC records

Quantified above — −0.114 AUC on near-debutant matchups — but not yet fixed. Tapology and Sherdog are already in the collection step; joining them cleanly to canonical fighter IDs is the work that remains.

A better calibrator

Isotonic on 504 validation fights emits 24 distinct values. Beta calibration might return the resolution that step function throws away. It is untested here, so it is unclaimed here.
