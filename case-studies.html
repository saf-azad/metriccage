Case studies

A backtest on historical data is a laboratory result. Each card below was forecast before it took place and scored against what actually happened.

UFC Fight Night — Macau · 2026 · featured

Of the thirteen bouts on the card, eight were scoreable. Four were UFC debutants, whom the model does not attempt to rate because they carry no record in the feature matrix, and one ended as a no-contest.

On the eight it scored, the model took five — against an expected 5.33 from its own probabilities. That result sits in the middle of the central 80% range it had predicted for itself, which is exactly what a calibrated forecast is supposed to do: the number it gave in advance described the outcome it went on to produce.

The card is also a clean demonstration of the model declining to guess. Rating the four debutants would have been easy and would have padded the count; leaving them unscored keeps the record honest and keeps the expected-versus-actual comparison meaningful.

Calibration is a claim about the long run, and it holds up card by card too.

Five against 5.33 expected is not a lucky result or an unlucky one. It is the forecast landing where the forecast said it would.

Method

How these are scored

A card is only evidence if the forecast existed before the fights did. Each set of predictions is generated and stored ahead of the event, using the feature matrix as it stood at that moment — no post-hoc feature updates, no re-running with hindsight, and no dropping a bout after the fact.

Bouts the model cannot rate are marked unscoreable rather than filled in. A UFC debutant carries no history in the feature matrix, so any number attached to them would be the betting market’s opinion rather than the model’s.

A pick counts as correct when the fighter given the higher win probability wins. No-contests and overturned results are excluded from the denominator rather than counted either way.

Card records are raw counts, never percentages.

Five out of eight is five out of eight. A card of this size cannot support a percentage to two significant figures, and the interval around one would be wide enough to contain almost any reading you cared to take from it.

The aggregate metrics on the results page — computed across 695 fights — are the numbers that carry statistical weight. These cards show the model working in the open, on events as they happen.
