This page used to say the model went 9 for 13. It did not: of the thirteen bouts, one was a no-contest and four were UFC debutants the model cannot score at all, so five of those nine picks were market-led. On the eight it actually scored it went five — against an expected 5.33 from its own probabilities (central 80%: 4 to 7).

Lesson. A calibrated model can still be confidently wrong about a population it has never seen — calibration is measured in aggregate; coverage gaps hide inside it.

Method

How these were scored.

Each card was predicted before it took place, using only data available at that moment — no post-hoc feature updates, no re-running with hindsight. A pick counts as correct when the fighter given the higher win probability wins.

Card records are raw counts, never percentages. Nine out of thirteen is nine out of thirteen; it is not “69% accuracy”, and treating it as such is exactly the overclaiming this project is built to avoid.

The aggregate metrics on the results scoreboard — computed across 695 fights — are the numbers that carry statistical weight. These cards are here for what they reveal about failure modes.
