The calls that landed

Eleven times it overruled its own rating system

The hardest thing a fight model can do is disagree with the consensus and be right. MetricCage carries its own consensus in the form of an Elo rating (one of its four streams), and the cleanest test of whether the other three earn their place is how often the blend overrules Elo and wins. In 2026 that happened eleven times at 60 per cent conviction or better.

Elo is a strength-of-schedule measure and nothing more.

It cannot see that a fighter is thirty-eight, or coming off a long layoff, or stylistically wrong for the man in front of him. The other three streams can, and these eleven are where they paid for themselves.

UFC Fight Night main event · 14 March 2026

Vallejos v Emmett

called · fighter and shape

Josh Emmett, long-established; Kevin Vallejos, sixteen years younger and thirty-eight career rounds lighter.

Elo made Emmett the marginal favourite at 52 per cent. The ensemble put Vallejos at 69.6, and the drivers it reported were the age gap, a shorter layoff, and a positive net striking differential. Then the method head split that 69.6 with knockout on top at 26.8 per cent, ahead of the decision branch, unusual in a five-round fight, where the decision branch is normally the safe bet.

Vallejos knocked Emmett out with punches at 3:33 of round one.

Vallejos v Emmett as the model priced it

UFC 327 main event · 11 April 2026

Prochazka v Ulberg

called · fighter and shape
53.7% Ulberg by knockout, the model’s highest single method probability of the season

The strongest method call the model produced all year: Ulberg at 67.4 per cent to win, and 53.7 per cent to do it by knockout. No other fight in 2026 drew that much method conviction.

Ulberg knocked Prochazka out with a punch at 3:45 of the first round. Two five-round main events, four weeks apart, where the model had both the fighter and the shape.

One caveat, applied honestly.

The method head has no measurable edge across the season as a whole. These two calls were right, and right in the way the model intended, but they sit inside a distribution that does not beat a simple rule. Two good calls are two good calls.

Reading the card

Three calls worth anything

Only three bouts clear the 65 per cent bar, and on the season’s evidence they are the only ones worth anything: Salkilld at 67.3 per cent over Gamrot in the main event, and Nurgozhay and Juliana Miller both at 69.6. Everything else on the card is inside the range where the model is close to a coin.

The main event is the boldest of the three. Backing Quillan Salkilld over Mateusz Gamrot is a strong statement about a fighter with four UFC bouts against an established contender, and the model has already gone out on this limb twice in 2026 and was right both times: Salkilld submitted Jamie Mullarkey in January and knocked out Beneil Dariush in May.

The method columns should be read as decoration rather than information. The two submission-first estimates on this card, Salkilld and Thainara, are exactly the kind of call the head got wrong all season. If you want a method prior, the base rate is the better one: expect a little over half of these to reach the judges.

The model also flags unusual uncertainty here.

Every fight it could price triggered a stream-disagreement warning, with the four models spreading between eleven and thirty-three percentage points, which reflects a card built largely from fighters with short UFC histories.

Calibrated probability estimates from a model with documented limits, running on data that stops three months before the card. Not betting advice. About a third of the calls will miss; the value is in the number attached to each one.
