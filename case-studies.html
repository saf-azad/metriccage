Case studies

Testing a model on old fights is a laboratory result. Each card below was forecast before it happened, then scored against what actually did — which is the version nobody can tidy up afterwards.

Thirteen bouts were on the card and eight of them could be scored. Four involved UFC debutants, whom the model will not rate because it has no record of them to read, and one ended as a no-contest.

Of the eight it did score, the model called five. Its own probabilities had said to expect 5.33. That lands in the middle of the central 80% range it had predicted for itself, which is exactly the point: the odds it published in advance described the result it went on to produce.

The card also shows the model declining to guess. Rating those four debutants would have been easy and would have padded the count. Leaving them alone keeps the record honest — and keeps the comparison between expected and actual worth making at all.

Honest odds are a claim about the long run — but they show up card by card too.

Five against 5.33 expected is neither a lucky night nor an unlucky one. It is a forecast landing where the forecast said it would.

Why debutants get left out

Measured across the whole 695-fight blind test rather than one card, split by how much UFC history the matchup carries. Higher is better; 0.500 would mean guessing.

Method

How these are scored

A card is only evidence if the forecast existed before the fights did. Every set of predictions is produced and stored ahead of the event, using the data exactly as it stood at that moment. Nothing is updated afterwards, nothing is re-run with the benefit of hindsight, and no bout is quietly dropped once the result is in.

Fights the model cannot rate are marked unscoreable rather than filled in with something plausible. A UFC debutant has no history for the model to read, so any number put beside their name would really be the betting market’s opinion wearing the model’s clothes.

A pick counts as correct when the fighter given the higher win probability wins. No-contests and overturned results are removed from the count altogether rather than being scored one way or the other.

Card records are reported as plain counts, never percentages.

Five out of eight is five out of eight. Turning it into a percentage would borrow an air of precision it has not earned: eight fights cannot support a figure carried to two decimal places, and the honest margin around one would be wide enough to cover almost any conclusion you cared to draw from it.

The figures on the results page, computed across 695 fights, are the ones carrying real statistical weight. These cards are here to show the model working in the open, on events as they happen.
