Every number below is computed, not quoted — from the public round-by-round record of every UFC fight since 1993. In August 2026 all of them were recomputed against elapsed fight time, because both the denominator and the total volume roughly tripled across this window, and four published findings did not survive it. The corrections are the first thing on this page, not a footnote at the bottom.

Discovery 00 · the correction that reframes the rest

A share of a tripling total
is not a trend.

Between 1994 and 2026 the mean fight got longer and the strike output per minute nearly tripled. Every era comparison on this page used to be a share or a per-fight count, and in a sport where the denominator moves that far, a share can fall while the thing it measures is rising. Flip the switch below and watch the clinch come back to life.

Why this one control changes four findings

The published claim was that the ground “emptied out” and the clinch was dying. Both were read off shares. Per minute of cage time the ground did decline — by 38%, not 79% — and the clinch is busier than it was in 1997. What actually happened is that distance striking grew more than sevenfold and out-voted everything else, which is a larger and more defensible claim than the one it replaces, from the same three numbers.

Falsify it: compute combined significant strikes landed per fight-minute by era. If it were flat, shares and rates would agree and this whole section would be unnecessary. It goes 2.76 → 8.04.

adjusted and comparable.
